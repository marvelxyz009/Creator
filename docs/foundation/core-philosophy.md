# Core Philosophy

## Why this framework exists

Large language models, left to themselves, produce prose that is **competent, coherent, and forgettable**. They average toward the median of their training distribution. They resolve tension prematurely. They explain emotions instead of implying them. They reach for archetypes instead of contradictions.

AetherForge treats this not as a flaw to prompt-engineer around, but as a **structural property of generation** that must be countered with **structural process**.

## The Five Principles

### 1. The story is the spine, not the sentences
Narrative integrity lives at the level of theme, character contradiction, and emotional debt — not at the level of word choice. Sentence-level polish without spine produces "well-written nothing".

### 2. Contradiction is the engine
Characters do not have traits; they have **opposed forces**. A character who wants safety AND wants to be seen is dramatic. A character who is "brave and loyal" is furniture.

### 3. Imply, do not state
Emotion stated is emotion dissipated. The framework's revision agents are biased to **cut explanation** and **preserve the gesture, the silence, the misplaced object**. See [[ai-smells]].

### 4. Memory is structural, not textual
What happened in chapter 3 is not "in the previous text". It is a node in the [[memory-graph]] with relations, debts, and unresolved tensions. The Context Assembly agent draws from the graph, not from the running document.

### 5. Canon is earned
No generated text is canon until it has passed the [[anti-ai-revision-pipeline]]. Generation is cheap; canonization is gated.

## What this framework is NOT

- It is not a "story generator". It is a discipline for steering generation.
- It is not opposed to AI. It is opposed to **lazy AI output**.
- It is not a style guide. It is an architecture.

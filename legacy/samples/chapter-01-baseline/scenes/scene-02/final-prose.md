The coat was still on her when she sat down. The form was where she had left it. The harbor had gone quiet.

She put the kettle on without looking at the deadline. It was printed on the bottom right of the form, in the small font the Archive used for the things it would not say out loud. She read it once. She put the entry on the table next to the form and read the entry too. She had read both already, many times, but it was the only thing the hand knew to do.

The first shard was out of alignment. She did not fix it.

The kettle clicked off. She did not get up.

She put her hand flat on the table, palm down, and it closed once — slightly — the way a hand closes on a small brass thing. She did not look at her hand. She put it back where it had been.

The first shard she fitted with the side of her thumb. The second. The third. The kitchen was the same kitchen it had been for four years and she had cracked the tabletop the day she had opened the entry. She had not realized, until tonight, that there was a chip on the rim. She had probably done it the same day. The chip was older than the crack. The crack had been hers; the chip belonged to someone she could not name.

She fitted the last shard.

She read the deadline once more.

She did not sign.

She got up. She took down a different book from the shelf — one she had not opened in a long time, one Tomas had not given her — and she put the entry inside it, folded once, between two pages whose subject did not matter. She put the book back.

She sat at the table.

She composed the message. It was short. It did not name the entry, did not name the form, did not name Tomas, did not name herself. It went to a person who had answered three other letters in the past year that she had not sent. She had kept the address.

She sent it before she had decided to send it.

The kettle was cold. The form was where it had been. The cracked glass was whole except for a chip she had not previously noticed.

She stood. She took the coat off and folded it on the chair, not on the hook.

She left the form on the table.

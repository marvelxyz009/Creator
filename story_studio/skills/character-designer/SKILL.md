# Character Designer

Derive each major character from an active desire, a conflicting need, a fear and a strategy that once worked but now costs them. Specify what they will do under pressure and what they refuse to sacrifice. Backstory matters only when it changes a present decision.

Give each relationship asymmetric wants, shared history, leverage, affection or resentment, and a concrete point of rupture. Antagonists pursue their own coherent goals. Secondary characters have lives and competing obligations beyond supporting the protagonist. Let family and romance alter action and politics through choices, not detached interludes.

For each recurring character deliver: role, desire/need/contradiction, agency in current arc, knowledge and false beliefs, relationship changes, voice with brief contrasting dialogue examples, and a visual identity sheet. Use silhouette, posture, expression range, age, clothing, props and distinguishing marks; separate invariant identifiers from scene-dependent injuries and outfits. Avoid defining personality solely through appearance or demographic stereotypes.

Check whether two characters could exchange dialogue or decisions without changing the scene. Redesign overlapping roles. Map the change earned by decisions, including refusal to change; do not assume every arc ends in growth.

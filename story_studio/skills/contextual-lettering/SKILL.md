# Contextual lettering

Lettering is acting. Read the scene before selecting balloon type. Identify speaker, intent, intensity, cadence and whether they interrupt, hesitate, whisper, shout, think or speak off-panel. Ordinary dialogue needs restrained shapes; do not make every political disagreement a shout.

Break long utterances where their meaning pauses. Joined balloons can mark a continuous thought; separate balloons and space can mark hesitation. Tails point toward speakers without puncturing heads, eyes or hands. Speaker attribution must survive reading at intended display size.

Choose a comic lettering family compatible with the line art and Vietnamese coverage when needed. Hand-drawn art does not imply cursive handwriting. Maintain stable ordinary lettering while changing emphasis deliberately. Avoid mechanically identical ovals, giant empty strips, tiny text and random jitter as a substitute for design.

Specify per page/panel: exact string, speaker, balloon order, line breaks, shape, stroke, tail destination, emphasis and protected art. Preserve exact source-linked dialogue. Review whole-page eye flow, not isolated balloons.
